# Method catalog

This is a curated map of research families explored during the project. Related variants are grouped together; a row is not a count of independent trials or a claim that every configuration completed the same evaluation. The [experiment report index](../reports/experiment_index.csv) lists 131 report-backed records by title and source digest. Those records include variants, feasibility work, audits, and repeated research branches; they are not 131 distinct methods. Detailed aggregate comparisons that can be reported are in [results.md](results.md). Raw datasets, predictions, model binaries, and experiment workspaces are not part of this repository.

## Catalog

| Family | Hypothesis and approaches explored | Matched control or audit | Evidence status | Disposition and unresolved question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rolling statistics | Changes in mean, scale, quantiles, autocorrelation, and local contrasts may reveal a break. Multiple windows and history summaries were screened. | Keep the same windows and scaling, then compare with fewer low-order moments. | Historical screens; most candidate selection used shared development folds (`POST_SELECTION_CV`). | Useful diagnostics, but no universal window or robust advantage was established. How should window scale adapt without using future length or tuning on the evaluation set? |
| Conditional normalization and PIT | Fit a causal one-step conditional distribution and transform each observation to a PIT or Gaussian score. Explored AR, score-driven/GARCH, copula, empirical-CDF, and tail variants. | Compare raw observations and conditional innovations on the same stream; inspect residual dependence and calibration. | The public Gaussian AR(1) normalizer is tested for prefix behavior. Historical variants have mixed provenance. | Retained as an interpretable representation, not a guarantee of independent or uniform residuals. Which conditional models remain calibrated under heterogeneous drift and heavy tails? |
| Sequential evidence | Accumulating weak deviations with CUSUM, restart, one-change hazard, betting, martingale, or predictive-rank evidence may improve detection. | Use the same per-step input and independently calibrate thresholds on null streams; report false alarms and delay. | A simple uncalibrated CUSUM is in the public reference. Historical sequential branches include partial and selection-exposed evaluations. | Keep the simple channels as an example. A useful evidence score still needs protocol-specific threshold calibration and dependence analysis. |
| Kernel methods | Kernel discrepancies, online kernel CUSUM, random Fourier features, and dependence statistics may detect nonlinear distribution changes. | Match the stream, windows, normalization, and null calibration; compare with conditional moments and simple CUSUMs. | Historical Trial 11, a system separate from the current public API, has an exact-stream package replay and verified official aggregate score, while its CV mean is selection-exposed. Sequential RFF-MMD package evaluation was incomplete (`PARTIAL_FOLD`). | The kernel reference was a strong measured competition candidate, but no public-data generalization claim follows. Which kernels and bandwidths transfer across series? |
| Spectral and multiscale features | Frequency, bispectral coupling, wavelet, long-memory, and time-frequency changes may expose shifts missed by location and scale summaries. | Match sample windows and compare against low-order time-domain summaries. | Several causal screens; no complete clean cross-domain result was selected. | Not included in the public baseline. Robust behavior under short, noisy, irregular streams remains open. |
| Dynamics and operator methods | DMD/Koopman modes, Markov transitions, sparse transition equations, local AR parameters, and score-driven dynamics may change at a regime boundary. | Match embedding, window, scaling, and rank against low-order features; use a frozen diagnostic in addition to CV. | The DMD/Koopman research blend had the highest selected CV-A mean in the local summary but a lower reduced diagnostic (`POST_SELECTION_CV`). Bayesian and score-driven variants showed a similar CV/reduced gap. | No family-wide rejection. Configuration sensitivity and transfer to untouched data remain unresolved; see [the aggregate table](../reports/curated_results/historical_research.csv). |
| Path and geometric summaries | Lead-lag signatures, rough paths, recurrence geometry, visibility graphs, matrix profiles, persistent Laplacians, and subspace features may retain order and path shape. | Use the same windows and feature budget with low-order moments or autocorrelation controls. | Signature screens covered only a subset of folds and showed very small, inconsistent deltas (`PARTIAL_FOLD`). Other variants lack a full reproducible benchmark. | Interesting for structured dynamics, but cost and tuning were not justified by available evidence. Do path features help after a properly nested, full-protocol comparison? |
| Density ratios and transport | Conditional RuLSIF, direct density ratios, Wasserstein distances, and self-normalized comparisons may distinguish pre- and post-change distributions. | Match the reference/online sample counts and windows; measure calibration and prevent sample reuse across meta-training folds. | A later CRM/RuLSIF blend has `NON_NESTED_META_CV` and `POST_SELECTION_CV`; it has no reduced or package confirmation. | Do not interpret its selected CV mean as an independent estimate. Standalone ratio and transport methods need nested evaluation and calibration. |
| Bayesian change models | Run-length posteriors, context trees, conjugate predictive likelihoods, and one-change hazards may represent uncertainty over break timing. | Sensitivity analysis for priors and hazards; compare compute and detection delay against sequential CUSUM. | A continuous Bayesian-AR context-tree cross-fit is recorded in the historical table as `POST_SELECTION_CV`; no public independent benchmark exists. | Priors can encode strong assumptions and affect the result. Robust prior selection without holdout adaptation remains open. |
| Conformal and exchangeability methods | Weighted conformal scores, restart martingales, Venn-Abers calibration, and related e-values may support online change evidence. | Hold the nonconformity model fixed and test the relevant exchangeability/dependence assumptions; match null calibration. | Explored as research variants, not a complete calibrated reference detector. | No claim of distribution-free validity is made for dependent streams. Which dependence-aware calibration is practical online? |
| Learned representations | Temporal-neighborhood coding, whitened reference encoders, contrastive objectives, predictive coding, and learned scoring may transfer structure across series. | Same outer/inner folds, no overlap between representation fit and held-out groups, and matched low-dimensional controls. | Historical Trial 52, separate from the current public API, is a full exact-package replay (`VALID_EXACT_STREAM`) with `POST_SELECTION_CV`; other encoder pilots were incomplete or lacked a full package result. | Not promoted to the public core. Better transfer evidence requires nested fits, frozen data provenance, and a sealed final test. |
| Neural density and score models | Denoising score matching, probability-flow MMD, Stein scores, and posterior inference may characterize changes without a simple parametric likelihood. | Compare against a matched Gaussian/AR residual baseline and account for compute and calibration. | Pilot screens only; no clean full-protocol result is published. | Training stability, sample efficiency, dependence, and threshold calibration remain unresolved. |
| Foundation models | Forecast residuals or pretrained time-series/tabular representations might offer reusable features. | Pin checkpoint/version and pretraining provenance; compare to compact conditional models on the same prefix and folds. | Feasibility and pilot work only; no reproducible clean result is included. | Forecasting or tabular benchmark performance does not prove structural-break transfer. Model/data licensing and pretraining overlap also need review. |
| Ensembles and automated search | Pairwise/ranking heads, feature blends, stacked corrections, tree models, TPE/MBO, and portfolio selection might combine weak signals. | Nested OOF for every upstream stage; account for all trials and reserve an untouched holdout. | Some results are `POST_SELECTION_CV`; the CRM/RuLSIF blend is additionally `NON_NESTED_META_CV`. | Most sensitive to validation design and researcher degrees of freedom. Use nested selection and report the search budget, not just the winning score. |

## How to read status

- `VALID_EXACT_STREAM` applies to the recorded inference path: each score used only the reference and observed prefix. It does not certify model selection or fold independence.
- `POST_SELECTION_CV` means the same CV evidence informed repeated candidate choices and the reported score.
- `NON_NESTED_META_CV` means outer-fold information could reach meta-training through an upstream fitted stage.
- `PARTIAL_FOLD` means a planned full-fold comparison did not complete.
- `INVALID_FUTURE_LENGTH` marks historical inference/calibration paths that used the final unseen stream length.
- `UNKNOWN` means the available record does not justify a stronger validity claim.

See [validation](validation.md) for precise definitions and [failed experiments](failed_experiments.md) for the main controls and negative findings. No method family is declared disproven by this project.
